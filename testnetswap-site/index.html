How it works

Setup

You pick a pair and amount. Your browser and the maker agree the rate and two refund deadlines over a relay that only passes messages. It holds no keys or coins.

You lock

Your browser locks your coins in a hash-timelock contract (HTLC) on the send chain. Only you can refund it after your deadline.

They lock

The maker sees your funded contract and locks the other coin in a matching HTLC on the receive chain, with a shorter deadline.

You claim

You claim the maker's coins by revealing a secret. That same secret lets the maker claim yours. Swap complete, atomically.

The worst case if anything stalls: you reclaim what you locked, minus network fees, after your deadline. A maker can never take your coins: it can't claim yours without revealing the secret that lets you claim the maker's coins. Read the details →

What this is

Non-custodial & atomic

The service never holds your funds. Coins are only ever in a contract that completes the swap or refunds to you.

Testnet only

tLTC, tBTC and tXMR/sXMR are worthless test coins. This is infrastructure for builders, not a market. The rate is a fixed nominal value.

An open maker network

Anyone can run a maker and join the network, and you pick which one fills your swap. Your safety never depends on the choice. Pairs are tLTC↔tBTC, plus tXMR/sXMR→tBTC (or tLTC where a maker offers it).

Monero (tXMR / sXMR → tBTC or tLTC)

Monero swaps are experimental and run locally in your browser, where a maker offers them, using a cross-curve adaptor-signature protocol instead of the HTLC design. See how it works. Need test coins first? Use the faucet.
